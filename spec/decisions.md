# Decisions and assumptions

2026-10-15 issue #2 D31 (refines D29): Clear-sky planning scenarios (not verified forecasts) may take a departure direction from one mission-named NAVCEN SOLAR hazard notice when no source states a heading. The direction is the bearing range from the pad that points toward every downrange hazard area at once (15-90 degrees after outward 15-degree rounding); the whole range is swept with the generic ascent envelope, never a centerline. Notices whose areas do not share a range (alternate, recovery or dogleg areas) give no estimate. The same common range narrows a published heading before the any-area overlap is used. Hazard-only verified-forecast rules (R004, rules.md row 2) are unchanged: assessment mode stays route_screening with no timed forecast windows. Checked against 331 East Coast SOLAR notices on 2026-10-15 (evidence/build/flight-plan-audit/hazard-consistency-2026-10-15.json): 302 gave a common range (235 of them 30 degrees wide, 53 of them 15 degrees) and 29 did not; the ranges matched known azimuth families (ISS about 30-60, Starlink shells 105-135, GTO 75-105).

2026-10-14 issue #1 D29 (supersedes D27): The family-based Dragon/ISS northeast simulation is removed; no vehicle family, payload, destination or orbit establishes a departure direction. Every catalog mission enters automatic route discovery regardless of vehicle or date precision, with a per-source state (found, stale, not_found, ambiguous, unavailable, blocked, queued, pending) that never stays pending after a failed check. Sources: Next Spaceflight public mission pages (reported departure heading, treated as a 90° quadrant because the embedded numeric field showed only 45 or 135 across seven checked Florida missions) and US Coast Guard NAVCEN SOLAR launch hazard notices (hazard corridors only). Association is exact after explicit name normalization, plus pad validation (Next Spaceflight) or notice-window coverage of the scheduled date (SOLAR). A heading that does not overlap any published hazard area is withheld; when they overlap, only the overlapping bearings are swept (the hazard area never becomes a centerline). `published-heading-envelope-v1` combines an admitted heading with a generic, explicitly assumed ascent envelope (five headings × three heights) and observer geometry. It reports viewing direction from the observer separately from the departure heading. It gives an absolute watch time only for exact/window launches, otherwise minutes after liftoff. It treats paths below 3° as horizon-grazing. It assumes clear skies, so cloud forecasts are a secondary note and no longer remove a launch from Viewable. Hazard areas, headings and modeled estimates stay distinct; nothing becomes a timed path. Next Spaceflight publishes no terms or API permission: only the compass word, URL, hash and retrieval time are kept, requests are budgeted at 12/hour, and the adapter is disabled if the operator objects. Paid sources, Flight Club, ULA scraping (terms) and ISSC (HTTP 403) remain excluded. Audit: evidence/build/flight-plan-audit/STATUS.md.

2026-10-14 issue #1 D30 (refines D29): Route sources publish only days before launch (SOLAR notices; Next Spaceflight's short upcoming index), so route information is expected only for missions dated within 7 days (exact, window start or date-only label) or already flown. Flight-plan records carry a read-time `publication_expected` flag; `not_found` outside the window is labeled “Route not published yet” instead of “No published route found”. Coverage reports a `notice_window` denominator alongside the all-mission totals. Discovery itself is unchanged: every mission is still checked.
2026-10-13 follow-up D28: Viewable is the default inventory filter and uses location-owned, paginated viewing summaries, not vehicle names or schedule presence. Reuse assessment results for registered evidence and the nominal model for supported families; skip expensive empty assessments for unsupported entries. A changed point invalidates prior summaries immediately, and errors never expose the full schedule as if it were viewable. All retains every schedule entry with a distinct availability label/style. Completed launches from the observer's current local day remain included when they have a plan. Old `lv_filter` defaults migrate to `lv_filter_v2`; later explicit choices persist. Observations and Privacy entry points are hidden at the user's request, while direct routes and deletion/observation APIs remain available.

2026-10-13 follow-up D27: The user explicitly requested practical prelaunch simulations instead of blocking the result on verified route/profile data. Add `planning_scenario`, separate from the admitted-evidence mode/advice/windows. The first family is Cape Falcon 9 / Dragon station launches, identified from explicit station orbit metadata or Dragon CRS/Crew naming. Model a nominal northeast ascent with authored altitude/downrange knots, nine combinations of heading/height, WGS84 observer geometry and a two-minute watch buffer. Its deterministic manifest hash enters assessment/cache identities. This is an assumption-based simulation, not telemetry, official guidance, a mission-verified forecast or a passed empirical gate. No postlaunch actual time or sightings affect it. Only forecast values issued AND fetched before the scenario cutoff can appear in its weather summary. Lead with direction/time; put assumptions and technical source diagnostics behind disclosures. Unknown times, stopped launches, unsupported vehicles/sites/families receive no automatic scenario. See the PRD product amendment and `packages/visibility/planning.py`.
2026-10-13; baseline 1.0. Updated PRD prevails over older paid-trajectory instructions.

Assumptions: unobstructed geometric horizon, observer altitude zero meters ellipsoid when not supplied (disclosed); source altitude datum must be verified or the profile rejected. No internet required for bundled calculations. Public source access can change. Free API access does not imply that every linked image has an unrestricted license. Reuse only data needed; link images unless their rights are verified. No source parser is “approved” just because a human or LLM can read the page.

## Implementation decisions, 2026-10-13

- D18: Add optional `official_intervals`, `supported_intervals`, and `alternatives` to the assessment DTO. The original reconstructed window object requires elevation and bearings; using it for official timing would invent unsupported geometry. Official timing stays separate, and reminder anchors use unrounded weather-supported intervals. Existing required properties and tests remain intact.
- D19: Python 3.12 is the supported application runtime; the current independent pyproj reference requires it. Packages and image digests come from actual registry resolution. Local Next.js uses port 3007 because an existing process occupies 3000; no existing service is stopped.
- D20: Public evidence limitations remain release blockers, including the unqualified Orbcomm archive and unclassified TOMEX+ map. Build proceeds across independent milestones. No schedule feed or synthetic route is treated as passing G-REAL.
- D21: LL2 status and precision IDs are normalized from archived live configuration responses. Status 9 means payload deployed/completed, 6 means in flight, 2 retains unknown/rough timing, and precision 5 means day. Audited status/recovery corrections apply only until the provider changes its source revision.
- D22: A later immutable registry revision supersedes prior revisions in the same identity family, including a later rejection. Same-revision candidate alternatives remain separate. Pending notices are invalidated on import and recomputed before delivery.
- D23: The existing N: workspace is a local fixed disk, verified through Windows storage metadata. Local encrypted restore is useful software evidence but cannot substitute for the required off-host destination, RPO or disaster-recovery proof.
- D24: Completed launches remain browsable as viewing reviews. Preserve supported geometry and source context while separating the model from immediate advice, prelaunch forecasts and reported sightings. Completion never creates missing path/profile data. Unknown provider time placeholders never define a local date group.
- D25: Observation submission requires the actual viewing timestamp. Explicit retrospective reports may precede the linked assessment; the server also classifies every assessment first generated after liftoff as retrospective. These reports are excluded from prospective labeled denominators and false-positive/missed-sighting metrics. Existing prospective timestamp validation remains in effect.
- D26: Weather point lookups are shared across owners. Erasure removes the lookup and raw point data only after the last referencing location is removed; deleting one owner's session cannot invalidate another owner's weather lookup.
